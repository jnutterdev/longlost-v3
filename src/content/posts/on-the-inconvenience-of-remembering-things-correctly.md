Memory is not an archive. It's a bad cover version of something you once loved — familiar enough to recognize, wrong in ways you can't quite name. I've been thinking about this since I found a mixtape I made in 1997 and could not remember a single reason why I put those songs in that order.

The tape itself is physical evidence that I once had a logic. The songs are all ones I loved. The sequencing must have made sense to whoever I was then — there's a through-line, some emotional argument being made from track to track. But I can't reconstruct it. The person who made that tape is inaccessible to me except through artifacts.

## What We Leave For Ourselves

There's a version of this that's about getting older, about the natural compression that happens to memories over time. That's true but not interesting. What's interesting is how unreliable memory was from the beginning — not just now, not just after decades of attrition, but always.

I've compared notes with people who shared the same experiences and found that we remember completely different things. Not just details — the entire emotional register of an event can be different for two people who were both present. One person's worst night is another person's formative memory. The event is the same. The record is completely different.

> We are not reliable narrators of our own lives. We are enthusiastic narrators who have talked ourselves into a version of events that we find livable.

## The Mixtape Problem

The thing about the 1997 mixtape is that I can listen to it now and feel something. Not what I felt then — that's gone, probably permanently. But something. The songs carry residue. The sequencing, even incomprehensible now, has a quality. Something got communicated, even if the sender no longer remembers what they were trying to say.

This might be the best we can hope for. Not accurate recall, but artifacts with residue. Objects that hold a charge even after the original intention has dissolved. The mixtape still works as a mixtape. The memory of making it is gone, but something remains. That something is real, even if it's not the thing you thought you were preserving.
