The brutalism isn't incidental. It's the point. Villeneuve understood that the future looks like a building that gave up halfway through.

Every utopian vision of the future is betrayed by its architecture. The gleaming towers, the clean lines, the implied order — none of it survives contact with actual human habitation. What survives is concrete. What survives is repetition. What survives is the structure that was never meant to be beautiful because beauty was a luxury that got priced out somewhere around the second maintenance cycle.

Blade Runner 2049 gets this. The LAPD headquarters looks like a parking structure that developed ambitions. The orphanage in the wasteland is pure Soviet functionalism left to bake in a dying sun. Even the "nice" spaces — the Wallace Corporation, Joi's simulated apartment — have this quality of beauty that's been manufactured to compensate for something missing.

The future, if there is one, will not look like a render. It will look like what we already have, but more so.
