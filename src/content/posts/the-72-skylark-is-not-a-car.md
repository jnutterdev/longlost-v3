Some machines exist to be driven. Others exist to be parked under a streetlight while it rains. The Skylark belongs firmly in the second category.

This is not a criticism. There are plenty of cars optimized for performance. The '72 Skylark is optimized for presence. It takes up space in a specific way — chrome catching light at angles that were designed decades before anyone thought about aerodynamics or fuel economy or any of the things that actually matter if you're trying to get somewhere. The Skylark assumes you're already where you need to be.

Someone drove one past me last Tuesday at around 11pm, and I stopped walking for a full thirty seconds. Not because it's rare, not because I'm a car person. Because it looked exactly right against that particular stretch of road, in that light, at that hour. The car was doing what it was built to do.
